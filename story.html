It started with a robot.

Before I defended networks, I built a machine and watched code bring it to life. This is how one led to the other.

Scroll to begin

01. Washington, D.C., 2017

Leading Ghana's team on a world stage

I was team lead of Ghana's national team at the FIRST Global Challenge: a team of girls from Archbishop Porter Girls' Secondary School, competing against teams from more than 150 countries. I wanted to understand how technology works, and build it myself.

02. Kumasi, 2018 to 2022

Learning how computers really work

That curiosity took me to Computer Science at KNUST, where I graduated with Second Class Upper Division. Along the way I mentored fellow students and taught robotics to school pupils, especially girls.

03. Accra, 2022

Into the Security Operations Centre

I joined CyberHawk Limited, a managed security service provider, as a Level 1 SOC Analyst during national service. Every day meant a stream of alerts, and learning to tell a real attack from noise.

04. Detection

Turning noise into signal

I investigated more than 500 high-severity incidents across more than six clients. Tuning our detection rules contributed to a 30% reduction in false positives, so real threats stood out.

05. Teaching

Bringing others with me

I mentored more than 15 SOC analysts, led the SOC for a full week, ran phishing simulations and designed Crack the Code, a security awareness game.

06. 2026

Cybersecurity Engineer

Promoted three times in four years. Today I build the controls themselves: SIEM infrastructure, web filtering, certificates and mail security, alongside threat intelligence and vulnerability assessments.

07. What comes next

From responding to anticipating

I want to lead threat intelligence and detection engineering, then move into security architecture, designing systems that are secure from the start.
